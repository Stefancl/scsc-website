Meet Sonia

People have always been the story.

Photography came first. Then came years spent learning about people — how children grow, how teams work, how trust is built, and how connection changes what happens in front of a camera.

Long before SCSC

A camera was never the only thing shaping how Sonia sees.

Sonia studied photography before going on to study sport science, psychology and the development of children in sport. She later moved into education, youth development and leadership — spending years working closely with children, families, schools, communities and teams.

Her work also took her beyond the classroom through Round Square and international service projects, including work in Thailand and Cambodia. Those experiences demanded patience, calm leadership, cultural awareness and the ability to connect with people quickly — often in unfamiliar environments.

None of that sits separately from photography. It quietly shapes the way she works now.

Behind the camera

Good photographs begin when people stop performing.

Sonia knows how to read a room, when to give direction and when to step back. She understands that young children need space, that adults often arrive convinced they are awkward in front of a camera, and that the best moments rarely happen because someone was told to smile.

The aim is not perfection. It is trust, movement, energy and enough freedom for people to become themselves again.

Formal study. Real-world experience. One way of seeing people.

Sonia's background includes formal studies in photography, psychology, child development, sport and education. Rather than treating those as separate qualifications, she sees them as different ways of understanding people — how they move, communicate, grow and connect.

That is particularly important when photographing children, families, schools, sport and people who do not naturally feel comfortable in front of a camera.

“The camera matters.
But understanding people matters more.”
